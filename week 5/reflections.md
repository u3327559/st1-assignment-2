# Stage 2 Lab Reflection

The AI tool used my functional requirements to recognize areas of missing testability. During the analysis, it noted that my initial functional requirements did not specify performance benchmarks (for instance, particular search speeds) and inquired about data security measures, leading to a decision to add access levels to distinguish between managers and staff. On the other hand, I found the tool to be overly prescriptive, suggesting too many features, such as Telehealth Video Conferencing and AI-powered Diagnostic Tools. Therefore, I declined these recommendations, as they failed to take into account the client’s explicit request to design a relatively simple and easily maintainable system for scheduling medical appointments to digitize paper records. After peer feedback, I changed the patient search user story to make it specific and testable, including clear and stringent acceptance criteria. Thus, this experience taught me that I should always rely on the client’s requirements and evidence rather than unsubstantiated assertions from the AI tool. Otherwise, I risk adding unnecessary features that increase project costs and complexity without value to the client.
